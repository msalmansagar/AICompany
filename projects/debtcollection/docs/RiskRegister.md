## Addendum 2026-09-17 — F1–F11 review decisions

| ID | Risk | L | I | Mitigation | Owner | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-25 | **Housing Loan statistical findings are generalised into BFD business rules.** The HL analysis is vivid and quantified (exposure flat across buckets, 17.6 % below one instalment, >2000 DPD inert), and it is the only real data the project has. The temptation in Phases 3–8 is to encode HL's shape as "the" Collection policy — which would silently give BFD SME/Corporate the wrong strategy, eligibility and exposure treatment. | H | H | The **cross-platform principle** is binding: HL evidence may set HL *default configuration*, strategy recommendations, data-model requirements, required configurability and test scenarios — never cross-platform policy. Every threshold lives in a Rule Engine ruleset named by `qdb_platformconfiguration` (`qdb_eligibilityrulesetcode`, `qdb_strategyrulesetcode`, `qdb_contactholdrulesetcode`), so HL and BFD configure materially different criteria on the same build. Exposure stays in the model (F9). BFD identity, facility unit and MIS contract are established from BFD's own artefacts, not inferred. Phase gates check that no HL number appears in source. | architect | Open |
| R-26 | **Eligibility / segmentation / contact-hold thresholds get hard-coded during the Phase 1–4 build.** `arrears < 1 instalment`, `DPD < N`, `>2000 = Recovery` and `QCB DEAD ⇒ stop contact` are all easy to write as a conditional and hard to find afterwards; three of the four are still unanswered business policy. | H | H | ADR-DCP-11: eligibility runs through `IRuleEngine` against a configured ruleset — no dedicated engine, no threshold in application source. Configuration pointers, not values, live on `qdb_platformconfiguration`. **Testing rule (TestingStrategy):** no test may encode a threshold constant; eligibility and strategy tests drive rulesets as fixtures and assert the *outcome mapping*, so a hard-coded threshold fails the suite rather than passing it. Code review checks for numeric DPD/arrears literals in Collection logic. | Dev / reviewer | Open |
| R-27 | **Contact-hold policy is unconfirmed while deceased customers hold 24.4 % of arrears.** 724 accounts across 556 customers are QCB DEAD, spread over every bucket (97 in 1-30, 210 in >2000); 81 are *Deceased not applied* and 246 carry no exemption at all. Until QDB confirms whether QCB DEAD alone establishes a hold, automated communication could reach an estate — a conduct and PDPPL exposure, not merely a defect. | M | H | The architecture makes a confirmed deceased indicator **capable** of immediately triggering a server-side Contact Hold / Special Handling rule before any automated or manual send, enforced in the Communication Service and the plugin guard — never by UI hiding, so React and background jobs cannot bypass it. Whether QCB DEAD *alone* is sufficient stays `TBD — Requires QDB Confirmation` (§10 item 18) and is **not** asserted in code. Phase 7 is gated on the answer; until then the safe default is configured hold with manual release. | QDB / Compliance | `TBD — Requires QDB Confirmation` |
| R-28 | **Duplicate generic configuration infrastructure is created in Phase 1.** `qdb_platformconfiguration` and `qdb_platformmapping` were designed before the sandbox sweep revealed QDB's own mapping/configuration entities (`qdb_attributesmapping`, `qdb_attributemappingconfiguration`, `qdb_parentattributemapping`, `qdb_form_submission_mapping`, `qdb_reportentitymapping`, `qdb_taskapprovalappfieldmapping`, `qdb_autonumberconfig`).
